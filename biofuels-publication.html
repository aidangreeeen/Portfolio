// Abstract

As the global energy landscape transitions away from fossil fuels, biofuels have become a structural component of the transportation fuel supply across road, marine, and aviation sectors. A critical but systematically underappreciated dimension of this transition is tribology, the science of friction, wear, and lubrication. In fuel injection systems, pumps, and precision components, the fuel itself functions as the primary lubricant, meaning that changes in fuel chemistry translate directly into changes in component durability, efficiency, and service life. This review provides a comprehensive, chemistry grounded account of biofuel tribology covering FAME biodiesel, renewable diesel (HVO), bio oils, alcohol fuels, Fischer-Tropsch fuels, and sustainable aviation fuel (SAF). It establishes how molecular structure governs surface adsorption, viscosity, tribochemical film formation, and boundary film performance, and evaluates friction and wear characteristics through systematic analysis of HFRR, four ball, and BOCLE data, and examines component level behavior in injectors, pumps, piston assemblies, bearings, and aviation fuel metering systems, and addresses materials compatibility including metals, elastomers, and hard coatings, and analyzes degradation pathways and corrosion wear synergy, and reviews additive technology and formulation strategies. Sustainability, test standards, and lifecycle interactions are integrated throughout, and a structured analysis of knowledge gaps identifies in situ tribofilm characterization, biofuel aged fuel tribology, SAF aviation hardware wear testing, and AI-assisted lubricity prediction as the most urgent research priorities. Conflicting findings in the literature are identified explicitly rather than reconciled by silent selection.

// Coverage

Topics Covered

01
Biofuel Types & Properties

FAME biodiesel, renewable diesel (HVO), bio oils, alcohols, Fischer-Tropsch fuels, SAF, and the fuel properties that govern lubrication.

02
Fundamentals of Fuel Tribology

Boundary and mixed lubrication, lubricity vs. viscosity, film formation, tribochemistry, additives and contaminants.

03
Friction & Wear Behavior

Comparison with fossil fuels, and the effects of blend ratio, fuel aging, temperature, and load.

04
Engine & Aviation Components

Injectors and high-pressure pumps, piston rings and valvetrain, bearings, and aviation fuel metering at cold start.

05
Materials Compatibility

Metals and alloys, elastomer and seal degradation, corrosion–wear synergy, and surface coatings.

06
Degradation & Stability

Oxidation and deposit formation, moisture absorption, and long-term aging and storage.

07
Additives & Formulation

Lubricity improvers, nanoparticle and bio-based additives, interaction with engine oils, and regulation.

08
Sustainability & Testing

Efficiency, emissions and engine life, HFRR / four-ball / BOCLE test standards, and life-cycle impact.

09
Future Outlook

Knowledge gaps, advanced surface engineering, AI-assisted fuel and tribology optimization, next-generation biofuels.
